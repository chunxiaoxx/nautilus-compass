\begin{abstract}
Compass is an open-source memory recall pipeline for LLM agents that,
unlike Mem0, Letta, Cognee, Zep, MemOS, and smrti, does not call an LLM
to extract facts or build a graph at index time; raw conversation text
is embedded directly with BGE-m3. On LongMemEval-S (n=500), Compass
reaches \textbf{56.6\% accuracy} using DeepSeek V3.2 thinking, the
upper end of open-source same-setup baselines (50--60\%) and $+12$
points over the Gemini-2.5-pro baseline (44.6\%). Recent white-box
leaders (OMEGA, Mem0g, ByteRover) report 90+\% on the same benchmark;
we treat that gap as the architectural ceiling of the no-extraction
design rather than a tuning gap. End-to-end reproduction cost is
\$3.50 on Volcengine DeepSeek pricing (regional; offshore providers
$5$--$10\times$ that, still well under GPT-4o-judged stacks at \$50+).
On EverMemBench-Dynamic, Compass scores \textbf{44.4\%} (n=500, Run 1)
and \textbf{47.3\%} (n=497, Run 2); both runs exceed all four published
Table 4 baselines (MemoBase, Mem0, Zep, MemOS, top at 42.55), with
across-run mean 45.84\% clearing MemOS by $+$3.3 pts. We do not claim
industry SOTA: OMEGA and Mem0g have not published comparable
EverMemBench numbers. Our 5-stage pipeline combines BGE-m3 dense recall,
bge-reranker-v2-m3 reranking, multi-angle query rewriting, type-aware
prompting, and cross-judge replication ($\kappa{=}0.772$). Multi-angle
query rewriting is the dominant lever ($+27$ pts on
single-session-user), larger than the reranker. Across 6 LLMs,
thinking-mode effectiveness varies: DeepSeek V3.2 $+10$ pts; GLM-5.1
$+2$; MiniMax M2.7 triggers a 44\% refusal cascade. Negative findings:
Neo4j graph reranking $-6$ pts on closed haystacks; n${<}100$ samples
over-estimated V4-pro by $+4.4$ points vs full 500. Code, MCP/A2A
integrations, full reproduction scripts at
\url{https://github.com/chunxiaoxx/nautilus-compass} under MIT.
\end{abstract}
