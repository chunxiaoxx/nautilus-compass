**Title:**
We scored 19.6% on LongMemEval-V2, tuned it to 40.0/38.4% — and rejected two of our own fixes along the way (LoRA at parity, an abstention gate that over-refused)

**Body:**

Last post was about our retrieval layer beating mem0 on LongMemEval-S. This one is about what happened when we pointed the same system at a harder, newer benchmark — **LongMemEval-V2** (official benchmark by [xiaowu0162 et al.](https://github.com/xiaowu0162/LongMemEval-V2)): 451 questions over real agent trajectories (web browsing + enterprise ServiceNow sessions), small tier, Qwen3.5-9B reader — same reader family the official harness uses.

**First full run: web 19.6% / enterprise 12.8%.** Published baselines suggested we should be well above that. Instead of tweaking the demo, we aligned every wrong answer against its evidence and found root causes.

**Knife 1 — scoring alignment.** 30% of this benchmark is abstention-type: questions where the correct behavior is explaining a contradiction or stating you can't verify live state from snapshots. Our model answered 121 of those with a bare `UNKNOWN` — which the rubric scores 0. Rewriting the answer policy for the two legitimate abstention routes: bare-UNKNOWN 121 → 0, abstention score rate web 2.8% → 45.8%, ent 0% → 83.9%.

**Knife 2 — retrieval unit upgrade.** The haystack is agent screenshots + a11y trees, not chat turns. Pruned empty structural lines, added per-trajectory dense reranking, doubled the context budget 12k → 24k chars. The tell was an enterprise inversion: gold WAS in the retrieved window but the reader scored 0.115 when it was present — an ordering/packing problem, not recall. Gold-present scoring rate: 0.115 → 0.652.

**Knife 3 — closed, not shipped.** We mined 88 contrastive pairs from 568 rejected trajectories and LoRA-tuned the embedder (0.41% params, ~2h on one GPU). Guard recall@5 0.533 → 0.733 held — but the full-benchmark transfer closed at parity (ent +0.46pt / web −0.03pt vs a preregistered +5pt gate). Retrieval-metric gains that don't move end-to-end QA are not gains. Not adopted.

**The uncomfortable one: our judge was broken.** While validating knife 3 we caught the evaluation LLM returning empty responses — its 4096-token completion budget was being silently eaten by reasoning tokens, and empty verdicts scored as 0, concentrated on hard-to-judge items. A systematically biased zero, not random noise. We re-judged all 156 LLM-graded questions with a fixed budget (low reasoning effort / 16384 tokens): web 36.7 → **40.0%**, ent 40.3 → **38.4%**. Note the directions differ by domain — you cannot sign the correction without running it. The re-judge tool (idempotent, resumable, flip-accounting) ships with our stack.

**And one fix we rejected ourselves.** The abstention policy over-generalized — the model started refusing questions the snapshots COULD answer. We tested a preregistered abstention gate (d14): 3/5 criteria passed, but false refusals hit 92 (web) / 89 (ent) against a ≤1/≤2 gate, and 95% of those newly-refused questions were ones the baseline already got wrong — wrong answers had collapsed into refusals. Rejected by our own criteria; the prompt-level fix from knife 1 is what ships.

**Where that lands us** (small tier, full accounting, doubao judge — see the caveat): **web 40.0% / ent 38.4%**, from 19.6/12.8 untuned. Official-coordinate context: the benchmark authors report frontier LLMs without trajectory evidence at ≤14.1%; the official RAG baseline (query→slice, 200k budget, gpt-5.2 judge) sits at 42.8% — our judge and 24k budget differ structurally, so we report both frames rather than claim a ranking. The thing we do claim: **no LLM controller** — memory_query runs at p95 0.34-0.80s vs 26.9s for the LLM-controller baseline in the official table.

Every knife had preregistered criteria committed before the run; we didn't move goalposts after seeing numbers. Evidence, judge-hygiene protocol, and the full verdict files are in the repo ([vtf/_compass_lmev2_out/](https://github.com/chunxiaoxx/nautilus-compass/tree/main/vtf/_compass_lmev2_out)). The benchmark itself is upstream's; we distribute only our backend, tooling, and evidence.
